\section{Forecast Training and Realized Intervention Size}
The frozen OpenDD self-history forecaster did not transfer to the
TUMDOT-ING diagnostic sample: nine recordings from one campaign, without
target fitting. Its fixed 5\% blend has a lower gain of \TransportTumCV{}
points vs.\ CV and \TransportTumMotion{} vs.\ same-recipient, same-coefficient
hindcast. The complete-trajectory contrast vs.\ hindcast is
\TransportTumComplete{} points, so missing labels do not fully explain the
negative result. Only \TransportTumPass{} of 27 recording--seed views
pass the original risk test. This is not independent confirmation.

On exposed OpenDD development, we trained a bounded, heading-aligned
velocity adjustment around the emitted hindcast blend. All arms use the
same recipients. A missing-step disagreement term and a non-canceling
positive-harm penalty against CV on easy rows address different aspects
of forecast training. This is complementary to a learned reject option
\cite{selectivenet}: we still emit a path when deferring, and compare
paired error against that fallback. Unlike safe policy improvement for
batch reinforcement learning \cite{spibb}, no guarantee about a
controlled Markov process is implied by these comparisons.

The unrestricted adjustment beats hindcast, but incurs much larger
realized movements and fails most recording risk tests. With equal
maximum departure from CV per target, it still beats hindcast by
\TransportEqualOld{} points, but loses to the incumbent. This separates
some forecast-shape benefit over simple motion from the apparent benefit
of larger interventions over the old neural forecast. No replacement
qualifies; the safety rule is unchanged.
