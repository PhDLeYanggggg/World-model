\section{Shared Displacement Budget in Hannover}

The earlier radius-matched comparison bounds each agent separately.
Here we pool the frozen half-radius budget within each recording and
seed. Let $c_{it}$ denote constant velocity, $o_{it}$ OLS4, $y_{it}$
the annotated target, and $V_i$ the observed steps. At row radius $r$,
the largest increase in lower utility against OLS4, relative to
predicting $c_{it}$, is
\begin{equation}
  f_i(r)=
  \sum_{t\in V_i}\min(r,\|c_{it}-y_{it}\|)
  +\sum_{t\notin V_i}\min(r,\|c_{it}-o_{it}\|).
\end{equation}
The first term projects toward the target; the second projects toward
OLS4 to reduce the missing-label disagreement debit. This relaxation
permits independent directions at each step and imposes no risk
constraints. The marginal returns of $f_i$ decrease at its sorted
distance knots. Filling the segments from highest to lowest slope
maximizes the sum under $\sum_i r_i\leq B$, where $B$ is the original
sum of row peaks. This is an attainable optimum only for the
label-aware, unconstrained-direction relaxation.

The finite-bank comparison instead permits optional frozen forecasts
and CV-to-OLS4 bridges on the original eligible recipients, with at
most one path per row. It keeps the original risk and easy support
rule, maximizes the smaller conservative gain against CV and OLS4,
and prices each chosen path at its actual peak deviation from CV.
Both objectives use observed CV error mass for normalization.

Even with arbitrary paths and all recipients, the optimistic OLS4
break-even is \HannoverRadiusBreakEven\ times the original total.
This is not an allowable intervention threshold. These ranges describe
three exposed clips from one acquisition campaign across three frozen
seeds; they are neither confidence intervals nor independent transfer
estimates. Reallocation alone cannot close the conservative gap on
this cohort. No new forecast policy is fitted or evaluated here.
