\section{Joint Choice of Recipient Slate and Edit Time}

For each legal spend count, a causal menu offers up to
five subsets, obtained from disagreement, large or small
candidate change, observed turning, and nearby agents.
The fixed-rank subset remains in the menu. For any subset
of the same size, the queue transition is identical; thus
the offline continuation is computed once per cardinality,
and individual targets restore each subset's immediate
return. The readout uses current and prior-query summaries,
selected and unselected agents, and relative predicted
separation on their edges. The latter is a forecast feature
in local coordinates, not a physical safety claim.

The learned rule changes \SlateQueueChanged{} recipients relative
to fixed rank at exactly the same query counts. Its paired
lower effect is \SlateQueueMain{}; the relative trajectory
effect is \SlateQueueRelative{}. The effect against immediate
spending of the same realized credits is \SlateQueueImmediate{}.
The full rule changes by \SlateQueueMyopic{} relative to the
myopic adaptive queue (unequal edit counts) and does not beat
the independently refitted no-geometry arm
(\SlateQueueGeometry{}) or CV (\SlateQueueCV{}).

At the original full per-recording totals, the outcome-aware
menu reaches \SlateQueueNoDelay{} joint passes without delay
and \SlateQueueTwoDelay{} with two-query delay. Separately,
keeping the learned policy's exact realized count in every
query, outcome-aware choice within the same menu reaches
\SlateQueueClockCapacity{} versus the causal
\SlateQueueJointPass{}. This second limit uses fewer
credits and is not a nested subset of the full-budget
limit. The residual gap at an identical clock cannot be
attributed only to timing. It does not certify the causal
ranker or the full recording roster.
