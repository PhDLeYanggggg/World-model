\section{Protecting reference under one query substitution}
We replace at most one query plan per recording, without changing
any query's edit count or slot radii. This makes the label for a
candidate substitution conditional on a fixed remainder of the
original plan. A local target records loss of selected easy
reference in the query; a whole-record target also requires that
no unchanged slot supplies it. Only training uses those outcomes.
In \CFSupportFits{} matched fits, both heads estimate a conservative
advantage over the anchor and differ only in the reference-loss
label. The policy takes the first query with a positive predicted
margin and estimated loss probability at most 0.20. This fixed
threshold is not conformal risk control~\cite{crc} or a certificate
of the original 2\% score.

Because only 5.7\% of donor options lose the whole-record reference,
we also train \CFSupportFactorFits{} follow-up models. They multiply
the local loss probability by the probability of whole-record loss
conditional on local loss. This arm reuses exposed development data;
the gate and outcome scorer do not change.

All policies use 1,457 edits across 606 recording--seed views.
Direct whole versus direct local is \CFSupportWholeLocal{} conservative
pp; the factorized product versus its own local gate is
\CFSupportFactorLocalContrast{} pp (95\% paired location intervals).
The product protects some references relative to its same-query gain
control, but does not increase dual passes. Direct local gains two
dual passes over past prefix while passing fewer risk checks, with
conservative utility \CFSupportLocalPrefix{} pp versus past prefix.

With outcomes known, one substitution is enough for
\CFSupportOracle{} views. The direct whole model fired on
\CFSupportOffered{} queries where the menu had a qualifying option
and picked one in \CFSupportHit{}. The gap is not solely a problem
with the risk score at an already chosen query. Hindsight capacity
does not change deployment or the independent confirmation status.
