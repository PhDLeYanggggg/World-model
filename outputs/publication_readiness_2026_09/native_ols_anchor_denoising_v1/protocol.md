# Does denoising the position anchor improve a causal OLS4 forecast?

Prior motion variants usually preserve the last observed position and alter velocity. This test distinguishes the position intercept from the velocity estimate. Keep the four-position OLS slope, but estimate the current position from the fitted line's endpoint rather than force that line through the final measurement. Compare the raw line, a fixed three-step ramp toward its intercept, and a learned fraction of that ramp. Never change an observed input or use a future coordinate in inference. Use standard last-step CV, anchored OLS4, and anchored OLS8 as controls.

For a slope control, move in the OLS8-minus-OLS4 velocity direction, scaled to the same maximum displacement as the position offset when both are defined. Train identical scalar-fraction networks for position and slope at three seeds (600 updates of 128 rows), with nine held fitting sources, paired draws, past-only covariates, masked signed ADE plus positive harm, and the original maximal-disagreement debit for wholly unknown rows. Do not tune a selector or choose an operating threshold. Also compare position and slope with the **same learned anchor fraction** on their common support and OLS4 elsewhere, so action counts and displacements match exactly, not just their upper caps.

Score whole-roster 8-observation/12-forecast paths, not deployed replacements. Primary: conservative learned-anchor gain against OLS4. Mechanism: paired common-support anchor vs slope at equal maximum displacement per row. Report CV contrasts, available ADE and step-12 FDE, easy ADE, worst source p95, and positive harm. Normalize conservative contrasts by known CV error mass; bootstrap 3,000 sources after seed averaging. No development targets, calibration or confirmation access, new official risk rule, metric or seconds claims, deployment, Stage5C or SMC.
