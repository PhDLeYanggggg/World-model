\section{Checked feasible combinations with frozen neural alternatives}
\label{supp:cv-joint-feasibility}
An upper bound that is positive does not establish a feasible combination.
We therefore maximize the smaller of the CV and OLS4 paired lower gains,
with an optional edit per row, a fixed cap per query, and all original
CV-relative risk and support constraints. The action set is either the five
analytic alternatives or their union with frozen source-excluded EqMotion
and PCT. Untouched paths remain CV. We prune dominated actions only within
the same row. An LP relaxation can prove a negative upper bound; otherwise
an integer solver proposes a witness. Each positive witness is reconstructed
as a trajectory array and checked by the original scorer.

The expanded menu supplies feasible combinations that the analytic menu
cannot express at the same count cap. This is capacity, not a causal
selection result: the solver uses future outcomes, and a timeout does not
establish infeasibility. No new training, calibration, or independent
confirmation is performed.

In a post-hoc diagnostic, we hold each confirmed new witness's recipients
and individual peak edits fixed. We allow the analytic directions to shrink
or extrapolate to those exact peaks. The analytic rematch reproduces a
positive witness on \CVJointRematchEight{}/\CVJointNewEight{} views at cap
eight and \CVJointRematchWide{}/\CVJointNewWide{} at cap 32. This indicates
that the extra capacity is not solely a larger displacement allowance.
However, the original expanded witnesses worsen observed pair-displacement
error against OLS4 on the changed-query subset (gain
\CVJointPairEight{} and \CVJointPairWide{} pixels). Thus marginal utility
and original risk feasibility do not establish multi-agent geometry or
collision safety; joint metrics address a separate requirement~\cite{jointmetrics}.
