# Why The Shared-C Second Opinion Was Not Enough

This is a post-readout algebraic explanation of the complete fixed Cprototype, not a newly selected strategy or independent validation.

The full-strategy known utility is benefit minus positive harm. Using the same full known floor denominator in each source view, the cross-veto loses benefit relative to count-matched self selection by +3.4211 [+2.0988,+4.8955]pp. It reduces known harm by +0.7031 [+0.3310,+1.1601]pp. Both contrasts use paired source-group resampling; subtracting separate CI endpoints would not give this interval. The complete nonempty-query primary is different and remains negative as registered. Do not add its differently normalized contrast to this full-strategy harm measure.

The veto therefore rejects useful predictions as well as harmful ones. Reduced harm alone did not buy better utility at a fixed query-level intervention count, and the full recording-risk contract also failed. The two models share Ctraining; this does not test a genuinely independent critic. Different retained amplitude mixtures are another limitation, not evidence of a new risk theorem.

This negative result rules out promoting this particular shared-C veto or scaling it solely because its harm numerator fell. It does not prove that independently fitted risk estimation, new causal information or all future dynamics methods fail. Those require their own source-excluded fitting/validation design. No Hprototype score or threshold search was run. The original primary/support failures and reserved-source boundaries remain unchanged.
