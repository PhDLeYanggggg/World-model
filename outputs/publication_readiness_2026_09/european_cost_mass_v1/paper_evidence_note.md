# Manuscript Evidence Note

A controlled source-development experiment examined whether mean-preserving
postprocessing repairs expected-harm estimates. We fitted two bounded slopes
per cost head using honest nested out-of-fold records, matching total and
easy-case harm moments under the original intervention envelope. The study
included432 scalar readouts, three forecast seeds, six overlapping locality
assignments, two forecast families and unchanged raw/origin-L2 controls.
Predictions were committed before outer-source scoring.

The moment readout failed the original expected easy-harm MSE criterion.
For full-input cost-only heads, mass matching versus raw yielded0 positive,
3 negative and3 overlapping-zero locality-bootstrap intervals; versus L2 it
yielded0/4/2. Coverage-log error improved in4/6 comparisons against L2, but
other guards and99 bounded easy-harm fitting constraints failed. Auxiliary
event-ranking signals did not become a uniform expected-cost improvement.

These findings distinguish empirical mean preservation from useful conditional
risk allocation and transport. They do not establish an independent safety
guarantee, trajectory improvement or a general impossibility result. Intervals
use3000 resamples of four localities, averaging seeds within locality; repeated
assignments are dependent and intervals are unadjusted. The data are exposed
source development. Independent selection, calibration and confirmation are
not part of this experiment. Raw detector pixels and native annotation steps
must not be presented as verified metric coordinates or seconds.
