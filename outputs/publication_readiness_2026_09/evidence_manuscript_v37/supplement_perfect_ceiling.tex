\section{An Upper Bound from Intervention Cardinality}
Let $b_{qi}\geq0$ denote the CV error averaged over released future
steps of target $i$ in query $q$. Set $b_{qi}=0$ for wholly unobserved
targets. With at most $K_q$ replacements, the positive improvement
in available-label ADE cannot exceed $\sum_{j=1}^{K_q}b_{q(j)}$,
where the errors are ordered from largest to smallest. Divide by
the requested target count of each query before aggregation. The
unknown-disagreement debit and risk restrictions can only reduce
the feasible conservative gain. This bound requires no assumption
about candidate capacity or predictability. For a direct upper
bound against OLS4 we subtract its observed gain and omit the
nonnegative policy--OLS4 disagreement debit.

At the actual joint cardinality, the perfect-switch ceiling is below
the OLS4 conservative score in all 15 source groups. The dense OLS4
reference has a different intervention count, so this is a capacity
comparison rather than a matched-budget ranking. Without the cap,
the outcome-aware finite-menu witness retains CV on wholly unknown
rows and chooses only observed improvements. It passes the unchanged
2\% rule in all 120 recording--seed views. This witness establishes
feasibility with labels, not that an inference-time controller can
predict which replacements to make. No new policy was selected and
the 30 confirmation groups remain sealed.
