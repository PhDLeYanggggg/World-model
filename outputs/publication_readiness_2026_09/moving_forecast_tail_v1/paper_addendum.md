# Development Addendum: Harm Concentration And Causal Tail Ranking

On the frozen MovingDroneCrowd++ SOURCE forecast bank, we tested whether a
positive-excess target improves the ability of causal features to rank costly
forecast interventions. Eight source-held ExtraTrees fits were compared with
eight verified signed-risk controls using the same120features and capacity.
All models reproduced by a full refit. These exposed-development comparisons
use one producer seed and dependent windows; they are not independent tests.

At10% query-balanced ranking weight, source-macro easy positive budget-excess
capture changes from19.196/3.419/38.228% to20.675/4.078/39.099%. The fixed
follow-up screen fails in all three contexts. Label-sorted capture reaches
99.880/99.990/98.798%, showing cost concentration, not realizable policy gain.
Full-amplitude proposals contribute93.036/53.442/84.346% of positive easy mass,
but this attribution is conditional on the frozen proposer and is not a causal
comparison of amplitudes. Prior negative amplitude controls remain relevant.

Fitting-source capture of60.2-86.9% falls to0-49.7% on excluded sources. Causal
motion ranges also vary strongly across sources. The existing feature scale
depends on outer neighbor geometry as well as ego history; an ego-history
frame is a subsequent testable representation hypothesis, not a demonstrated
repair or an established cause. The next comparison should preserve targets,
original safety/support rules and independent-role boundaries.

This diagnostic neither selects a deployment policy nor certifies safety.
