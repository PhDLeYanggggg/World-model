\section{Does A Short Backtest Identify A Better Path?}

Feature-based forecast averaging is established~\cite{fforma}.
We test whether retrospective error inside just eight observed
positions helps choose a twelve-step motion path. At prefix lengths
four through seven, each expert predicts the remaining observed
positions. Its errors relative to OLS4 become features of a
whole-path convex mixture. Each arm uses the same six analytic
paths, architecture, initialization and training draws. Zeroing
the feature blocks gives static and kinematics-only controls.
Nine fits use 69,746 targets from nine original fitting groups;
the 125,540 exposed targets in 14 disjoint source groups are
scored only after freezing all forecasts.

The equal-peak control preserves \PrefixExpertCount{} nonidentity
edits across repeated seeds, with identical recipients and peak
displacement from CV. The observed gain against the kinematic
mixture mostly vanishes. None of the mixtures passes the original
recording risk contract on any of the 102 repeated views.

For \PrefixExpertComplete{} complete targets we also take the best
expert by mean backtest error, without a learned mixture. Agreement
is the sign agreement of nontied expert-pair errors. At each lead
we compare that single prefix winner to OLS4 on the same targets.

Agreement is higher at lead 12 than at lead 1, but the selected expert's cost
advantage does not. Counting correct orderings does not weight
the cost of a wrong choice. This is a diagnostic on the exposed
roster, not a learned calibration rule, deployment result, or
independent confirmation.
