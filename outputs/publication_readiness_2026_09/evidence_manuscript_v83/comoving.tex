\section{Forecasting in a Comoving Frame}

To separate producer quality from choosing interventions, we fit
a compact deterministic EqMotion adaptation~\cite{eqmotion} on seven
OpenDD location-held folds with three seeds. No pretrained weights
or future-dependent head selection are used. Each arm has a fixed
head, two layers, width 32 and the same draws and loss
(\MovingEqFits{} fits). Centered inputs subtract the focal final
position. Comoving inputs use
\(
 r_{jt}=x_{jt}-x_{i0}-t v_i,
 \quad v_i=x_{i0}-x_{i,-1}.
\)
We restore the focal CV path after predicting the departure.
A common velocity boost cancels from the residual coordinates and
reappears in the restored forecast. Current neighbor ordering and
the focal-derived scale are unaffected. The no-peer arm masks
the same three nearest-neighbor slots before prediction.

The comoving peer model changes the paired lower gain by
\MovingEqFrameEffect{} versus the centered peer model and by
\MovingEqPeerEffect{} versus the comoving ego model. The ego
model beats OLS4 (\MovingEqEgoVsOLS{}), but not CV
(\MovingEqEgoVsCV{}). These direct paired bounds charge
missing-step disagreement and average seeds before the site
bootstrap. The compact adaptation is not a reproduction of the
published best-of-20 EqMotion benchmark.

At \MovingEqEdits{} frozen slots, neural offsets are rescaled to
the original peak displacement. Across \MovingEqViews{} views,
the original shape has \MovingEqAnchorPass{} joint passes and the
past-prefix donor control has \MovingEqPrefixPass{}. None of the
projected neural shapes exceeds the latter. The direct utility effect
of the ego-only shape is \MovingEqEgoMatched{} against the original.
All arms leave \MovingEqUndefined{} views with undefined risk.
These results do not support replacing CV or promoting a
neural intervention policy.
