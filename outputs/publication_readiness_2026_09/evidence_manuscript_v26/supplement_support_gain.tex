\section{Observation Support Is Not Intervention Utility}
We separate two learning problems on the held fitting sources:
predicting if a released annotation will be available, and
predicting whether an edit will lower error if it is available.
For the first problem, \SupportGainObsFits\ matched fits add only
past box, boundary and peer-visibility cues. This lowers the
stepwise missing-label Brier score from \SupportGainMotionBrier\
to \SupportGainQualityBrier. The factorized policy's full-grid
gain over OLS4 is $\SupportGainQualityPolicy$ points, using the
observed CV denominator. Availability is not physical occlusion.

We then freeze the support predictions and fit only the conditional
gain, training \SupportGainCondFits\ heads. The pointwise arm
regresses normalized observed gain at each lead. The rank arm
adds within-query pairs, contrasting two trajectories only on
their jointly observed steps. Its inputs do not include future
positions, support labels or the cached support probabilities.
At scoring, all active arms change the same number of agents in
each predicted-availability quartile within a query. These are
equal predicted strata, not equal realized masks.

Pointwise and ranked conditional gain errors are
\SupportGainPointError\ and \SupportGainRankError\ versus
\SupportGainMeanError\ for donor means. This is displacement-weighted
error on labeled steps, not trajectory ADE. Improved regression
does not guarantee a useful intervention at a matched budget.
Unlike generic expert deferral~\cite{mao}, our readout charges
unavailable trajectory steps and preserves support strata.
We do not extend deferral consistency guarantees to this setting.
No new calibration, confirmation or original risk pass is claimed.
