# SOURCE Bank Support And Counterfactual Geometry

## What Was Recomputed

Fresh SOURCE support arithmetic completed for all nine frozen forecaster
pipelines and all 444 repeated recording views in 57.987 seconds. A separate
80.575-second reconstruction matches every recording count and reproduces all
nine original mean-cost SOURCE prediction hashes. These are fitting diagnostics,
not held-out generalization or independent scientific confirmation.

The first verifier failed because JSON string recording keys were compared to
numeric recording IDs. The original diagnostic already compared native IDs
correctly. The verifier now joins on serialized keys while retaining native

tests. The failed private log is preserved; no original labels or results changed.

## Is The Current Action Bank Empty Of Safe Opportunities?

No, on these SOURCE banks. Every required recording view contains a genuine
known easy action satisfying both original 2% positive-harm ratios. With no
additional fixed-count or interaction constraint, one such action can supply
finite known selected-reference support for that recording. This is an
outcome-informed feasibility argument, not an inference policy.

Every view also has at least three strictly beneficial safe action occurrences
with causal floor-relative radius at least 0.1 image pixel. The smallest, across
recording views, of the largest beneficial safe radii is 28.6036 pixels. The
0.1-pixel sensitivity is descriptive, not a new eligibility rule or risk budget.
No action indices or oracle decisions are exported. Feasible bank support does
not prove positive policy utility, causal learnability or full-future safety.

## A Distinct Cost-Support Hypothesis

For a causal candidate path and its actual floor, let D be their maximum
pointwise displacement over the forecast horizon. Available-label ADE obeys
the reverse triangle inequality, so its native benefit plus positive harm is
at most D. This check passes on all observed SOURCE labels.

The existing decoder uses the larger of candidate-to-CV and candidate-to-floor
radii. This is a valid but sometimes loose bound, not a newly established bug.
Reconstructed predictions exceed the tighter actual-floor bound as follows:

| Scope | Legal action views | Predicted B+H above D, beyond descriptive 5ppm |
|---|---:|---:|
| All legal candidates | 5,381,784 | 106,686 |
| Neural candidate | 897,060 | 82,175 |
| Original selected actions | 238,108 | 32,870 |

Thus about 13.8% of old selected SOURCE actions have a predicted cost sum above
the exact floor-relative causal bound. The neural candidate's median loose-to-
tight radius ratio ranges from about 2.59 to 4.34 across producer pipelines.
These repeated fitting views are not independent observations.

This identifies a testable decoder-support mismatch, not the cause of failed
generalization. Tightening a valid bound might reduce fitting error, but can
also lower predicted harm and admit unsafe actions. Neither policy utility nor
risk improvement follows automatically. A controlled change must retain the
same SOURCE rows, preprocessing, query draws, objective, roles and original
complete risk contract, with a fixed primary contrast before its readout.
No such new model has been trained in this diagnostic.
