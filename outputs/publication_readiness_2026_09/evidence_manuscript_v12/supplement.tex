\noindent\textbf{Scope.}
This supplement reports exposed development evidence and explains the
registered evaluation. There are no independent outcomes. The task uses
eight observed positions and twelve requested steps at native annotation
stride one. All trajectory distances are in image pixels.

\section{Forecast Bank and Selection Controls}
The bank contains six causal motion forecasts and three learned
forecast families: fixed-head EqMotion, Past Context Transformer,
and a self-kinematic forecast. CV is both fallback and the risk
reference; OLS4 estimates slope from the last four positions and is
an additional accuracy comparator. EqMotion supplies a geometric
forecast architecture~\cite{eqmotion}; neither the adaptation nor
our scoring is a reproduction of its published multimodal benchmark.

For each candidate, a fixed ridge model estimates six quantities:
signed gain, positive harm, easy positive harm, reference error,
easy reference error, and the probability of a wholly unobserved
future. It uses displacements, changes in velocity, query size,
and forecast deviations from CV, all computable from past positions
and forecast paths. The motion scale is one pixel plus 6.5 times
the median norm of past displacements. Fitting averages the
per-source sufficient statistics and uses a ridge penalty of
0.002 except on the intercept. Scored development sources are
excluded from their fits. The pooled fits used for transport do
not use external labels.

Independent and graph arms first nominate one option per agent.
The shared-family control instead picks a single family for the
same number of switches in a query. The graph uses the undirected
union of four-nearest-neighbor links at the last observed position.
The geometry cost averages relative-path change over forecast
steps, divides squared deviations by the product of endpoint
motion scales, and weights the edge by the inverse square root
of the degree product. Its coefficient is 0.10. The additive
arm drops the interaction cross term. Searching over agent
pairs with nominated options is not unrestricted joint search
over all candidate forecasts; the separate exact-search
diagnostic expands that set. Only the screened arm can
discard the proposal according to fitted gain, excess harm,
and positive estimated easy reference. This local screen is
not the outcome evaluator.

\section{Recording Risk and Accuracy Are Different Estimands}
Let $p_i$ and $b_i$ be the policy and CV paths. On a row
with at least one labeled future step, $e_i$ is the mean
Euclidean error over its available steps. Let $b_i^e=e_i(b)$
and $h_i=[e_i(p)-b_i^e]_+$. A genuine switch has
$d_i=\max_t\|p_{it}-b_{it}\|_2>0$. The easy subset is
$E=\{i:0<b_i^e\leq 12.491607\}$ on observed rows;
the hard subset uses $b_i^e>61.717885$. Both pixel cutoffs
were fitted before the evaluation, not recalibrated on the
scored development or external data.

Within each recording let $S_K$ be switched rows with
observed future support, $S_U$ switched rows without it, and
$U=\sum_{i\in S_U}d_i$. The original three checks are
\begin{align*}
R_{\rm all} &= \frac{\sum_{i\in S_K} h_i+U}{\sum_{i\in S_K} b_i^e},\\
R_{\rm easy} &= \frac{\sum_{i\in S_K\cap E}h_i+U}
                     {\sum_{i\in S_K\cap E}b_i^e},\\
R_{\rm signed} &= \frac{\sum_{i\in E}(e_i(p)-b_i^e)}
                       {\sum_{i\in E}b_i^e}.
\end{align*}
The first two sums use unweighted selected rows. The last uses
the whole observed easy reference. The same wholly unknown
disagreement is charged in both positive-harm upper bounds.
All denominators must be positive and all three ratios at most
2\%. No switches or no selected easy reference is not a pass.
For two selected rows with reference errors 1 and 100 and
positive harms 0.03 and 0, the joint ratio is below 2\%;
removing the second row increases it to 3\%. This illustrative
counterexample is not a new risk rule or a data result.

By contrast, paired accuracy gives each row weight $1/N_q$
within its query of $N_q$ eligible agents. For policies $a$
and $p$, sum the observed weighted gain $e_i(a)-e_i(p)$
and divide by the same observed CV error mass. For wholly
unobserved rows, subtract or add the weighted maximum
disagreement between the two compared paths to bound that
gain. Existing partial-row averages stay fixed; their missing
steps are not imputed and the denominator is not completed.
Thus this is a row-support completion envelope, not a
complete-grid ADE bound for partially observed trajectories.

Masses are merged within source and seed before forming each
ratio. Seeds are then averaged within source, and sources
are averaged equally. A paired bootstrap draws 14 sources
with replacement 3,000 times. It is distinct from the
completion envelope and does not correct for adaptive
development on exposed sources.

\section{Relative-Path Error and Source Influence}
A pair-step is observed only if both agents have a label at
the same step. Across the three repeated views, \PairObservedSteps{}
of \PairRequestedSteps{} requested pair-steps are observed.
At the requested terminal step, the count is
\PairObservedEndpoints{} of \PairRequestedEndpoints.

For a pair $(i,j)$, compare each predicted relative vector to
the observed relative vector. On an unobserved step, the
absolute difference of the two errors is bounded by the
norm of the difference between the predicted relative vectors.
Sum the observed differences and the signed missing-step
bounds, then divide by the number of requested pair-steps
in that query. This uses a full pair grid, unlike the
row-support completion envelope for trajectory utility.
Query effects are averaged within source and seed, then
averaged across seeds and sources. This is a geometric proxy,
not a measured collision rate.

Table~\ref{tab:source} recomputes each mean after dropping
one source group. This is a descriptive influence check, not
another confidence interval or a rule for excluding scenes.
All three forecaster seeds remain together within each
retained source. An unsupported source-seed metric would
make that contrast unavailable rather than being silently
discarded. Positive values favor joint selection.
On observed pairs, joint beats independent in all 14
source averages. That advantage does not survive the
full-grid lower envelope. For joint versus additive,
the observed sign can change under a single omission;
the lower envelope remains negative under every omission.
A negative lower envelope means that improvement is
not established, not that every possible completion loses.

\section{Prior Work and Claim Boundary}
Multi-expert deferral already studies regression costs and
choice of predictors~\cite{mao}; the contribution sought
here is not the existence of a switch or an expert head.
Selective regression can worsen performance within particular
subgroups as coverage shrinks~\cite{shah}. Our positive-harm
ratio on switched agents within a recording is different
from subgroup prediction error. It also need not be monotone
as the switch set shrinks. We do not claim conformal control:
the expected-risk guarantee for bounded monotone
losses~\cite{crc} does not establish our empirical per-recording
checks on a new source. These distinctions motivate the
matched controls; they do not establish a new theoretical
guarantee or a strong forecast result.

\section{Sealed Evaluation and Replay}
The external protocol uses 15 calibration groups
(40 recordings) and 30 separate confirmation groups
(72 recordings). A calibration policy must switch at
least once, pass all 120 recording-seed checks, and
have a strictly positive paired lower-utility interval
endpoint versus CV. Confirmation proceeds only after the
winner and calibration replay are frozen. Its primary
comparison requires a positive paired lower endpoint
versus both CV and OLS4 and all 216 checks to pass.
Equal-count controls isolate utility on the same active
queries; an empty matched comparison remains undefined.
Groups are exclusion units, not verified independent
physical sites. No calibration or confirmation scores are
reported here.

The de-identified aggregate replay preserves all 14
development source groups and three seeds. It reproduces
two recording-risk tables and ten relative-path comparisons;
the source-influence audit additionally checks its input
contrasts against their frozen paired bootstrap results.
This is not reproduction of upstream training or inference.
No images, raw labels, weights, or future-generation
executables are included in the review replay.
