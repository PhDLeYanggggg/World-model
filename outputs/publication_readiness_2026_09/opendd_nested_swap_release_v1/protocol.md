# OpenDD: magnitude-aware swaps with disjoint release calibration

Keep the native 8/12-step vehicle proposals and seven OpenDD development
locations. For each unordered pair of excluded locations and each of
three seeds, fit one support-aware head on the other five locations.
Each donor's forecast producer excludes that donor and both excluded
locations, using the verified triple-excluded checkpoints. The donor
incumbent is deterministic: at the archived query-construction rule's
chosen query, take the two largest legal neural departures from CV.
Fit on all its legal swaps. The held and calibration incumbents are the
same archived learned additive pair for both new arms. Thus the donor
anchors are not fitted on their own labels, but differ in policy from
the evaluated additive anchors.

The sign arm ranks the legal one-recipient swaps by individual sign
probability plus half of relative sign probability. The magnitude arm
restores both signed lower estimates to native units, adds them with
the same half-weight, and may nominate only a positive score. Both
preserve two edits at the original query and the original stepwise
displacement. They share weights, inputs, donor roles, and candidates.

After freezing the past-only scores, calibrate separately in each
ordered direction on its calibration location. Use the original
largest tied-score prefix for which every candidate satisfies the
unchanged 2% recording rule, beats both CV and OLS4, and also improves
on the additive incumbent's conservative trajectory error. If none
qualifies, freeze no threshold. On the held location release only at
the frozen threshold; otherwise output CV. Keep the archived additive
release as a control using its original calibration rule (which does
not require improvement over itself). Empty release is not a risk pass.

Publish both the score freeze and the disjoint calibration freeze
before held scoring. Report counts, risk/support failures, effects and
paired location resampling. No new external outcomes, metric or seconds
claims, release-policy replacement, or OpenReview action.
