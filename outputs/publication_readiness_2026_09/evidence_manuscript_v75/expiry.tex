\paragraph{Pricing expired edits.}
We fit one head to predict direct gain, the probability of a successful
move, conditional gain for a move or expiry, and the effect of withdrawing
the original edit. The same release thresholds and causal scheduler
compare a direct readout with a conditional mixture and an asymmetric
expiry price. The latter never credits anticipated benefit from expiry.
The mean distance between the original edit and CV also bounds
withdrawal by the triangle inequality, including missing steps; this
does not bound the utility of a successful move.

The priced readout makes \ExpiryMoves{} successful moves versus
\ExpiryDirectMoves{} for direct, with \ExpiryCount{} expiries in either
arm. Its expiry contribution is \ExpiryPriced{} versus \ExpiryDirect{},
but its total paired difference against direct is \ExpiryEffect{}.
It matches the original anchor's \ExpiryJoint{} joint checks rather
than exceeding it. Distinguishing the downside changes which edits
are dropped, without establishing better full-policy utility or risk.
