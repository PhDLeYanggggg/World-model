# Diagnosis: The Policy Sacrifices More Benefit Than Harm

## Material Passport

Fresh accounting and exact replay over 108 frozen source-role groups. No
training or decision change. All inputs are hash-verified cached forecasts,
heads, source lineage and decisions from the causal-descriptor experiment.
This is already-exposed development evidence, not an independent test.

## Finding

Against the same-current-query-count control, the new policy loses benefit
worth **0.02026% [0.00677%, 0.03796%]** of control total error. It also reduces
harm by **0.00638% [0.00183%, 0.01187%]** on that same denominator. The net
contrast is therefore **-0.01388% [-0.02977%, -0.00224%]**, reproducing the
parent result. These are normalized contributions, not selected-harm ratios.

The unchanged utility predictor expects **0.01383% less utility** on the new
exchanged selections (interval -0.02399% to -0.00577%). Thus the existing
causal scores contain a utility-magnitude signal that the sign-only admission
rule does not fully exploit. This does not prove that utility optimization
will succeed: its predictions can still be wrong on particular sources.

Unknown labels are not imputed as safe. Both arms exchange 110.81 rows per
dependent view on average, but have different unknown counts. These are
repeated views, not unique independent samples. Sparse and zero-change views
remain in all locality summaries.

## Consequence

Another feature or risk-loss fit is not yet the most discriminating action.
First keep estimators frozen and test whether using expected utility magnitude
inside a same-query predicted-risk constraint recovers benefit. Compare
independent-agent admission, whole-query admission and joint reassignment.
The fixed 2% all/easy tolerance does not change. Any new rule must also pass
the real outcome-risk and easy checks; predicted feasibility is not safety.

This is a step toward the actual scene-joint research question, not a claim
that a greedy allocator is novel. The present cohort contains retained
simultaneous query rows and is not proved to contain every visible agent.
Complete scene coverage and interaction-consistency evaluation remain gaps.
