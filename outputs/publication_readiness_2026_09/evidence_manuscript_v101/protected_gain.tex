\subsection{Protecting the support edit}
At the original 943 edits, we freeze the support recipient and path, as
well as the gain query, count, and displacement radius. The protected
head may change the gain direction, but it may change recipients only
after the support edit is observed, including a co-observed query.
We train on double-excluded proposals, comparing identical candidate
menus with observed support context or with that context masked.
Support was observed for \ProtectedGainSeen{} of \ProtectedGainQueries{}
gain queries. No future trajectories are used at inference.

These are counts across 606 exposed recording-seed views. Recipient
protection reduces losses of previously defined easy references from
\ProtectedGainGainLost{} to \ProtectedGainProtectedLost{}, but does not
eliminate them: the original gain recipient can be the only observed
reference even when the support path is fixed. One reference was
restored, so the aggregate undefined count conceals the turnover.
The paired conservative utility effect versus the original is
\ProtectedGainPrimaryEffect{}. The matched context effect is
\ProtectedGainContextEffect{}. The guard makes \ProtectedGainGuardChanges{}
direction changes and no recipient changes; its effect versus the
original is \ProtectedGainGuardEffect{}. Intervals resample the
seven locations, averaging the three seeds within each location.

With future labels used only to compute a diagnostic ceiling, exact
witnesses at the original gain query pass on \ProtectedGainCurrent{}
views. Allowing that gain edit to wait, while holding the support path,
gain radius and count fixed and preserving already defined references,
raises this to \ProtectedGainDelayed{} (unrestricted delay:
\ProtectedGainUnrestricted{}). The ceiling does not account for what
an online policy can know about the remaining opportunity. Neither
learned policy qualifies for confirmation; the per-recording 2\% rule
and undefined-reference handling are unchanged.
