# What This Experiment Can Establish

The only new learned objects are fixed closed-form residual probes. The
original trajectory producers and neural cost estimators are hash-verified
frozen assets, not freshly trained models. The support phase freshly executes
the original Torch heads on their fitting rows. That is inference, not training.

Inputs to a new probe are causal motion/context summaries and causal predictions
from a frozen model. Targets are realized easy-harm costs on the fitting
localities only. All learned bins and response scaling use those fitting rows.
The outer locality cannot enter preprocessing or fitting. Independent selection,
reserved calibration and confirmation roles are not opened.

OOF refers to each row being scored by an inner model that did not train on
that locality. The common easy definition is a meta-supervised label derived
from all three meta-fitting localities. It is not an independent label for an
inner-held row and is never supplied to the inner model as an input. This
distinction and earlier developmental exposure remain visible in the protocol.

The study changes only predicted H_E, clipped to the original predicted H.
D, H and D_E stay unchanged, as do both trajectory rollouts. H/envelope and
H_E/H are bounded model scores, not calibrated event probabilities. A residual
MSE improvement would not by itself demonstrate lower trajectory ADE/FDE,
valid physical safety, a deployable policy or a new world-model contribution.

The source assignments overlap. Three seeds are averaged within locality;
uncertainty uses four paired localities per assignment, not the many correlated
windows. Six positive intervals would satisfy the registered development gate,
not provide six independent replications, multiplicity correction or a formal
noninferiority certificate. Repeated development experiments require later
independent evidence; freezing does not erase previous outcome exposure.

The fixed bins and ridge regression are conventional tools. Novelty must come
from demonstrated relative-risk estimation and scene-level decision behavior,
supported by relevant comparisons and independent evidence, not from this
post-processing technique alone. See the primary-source literature note.
