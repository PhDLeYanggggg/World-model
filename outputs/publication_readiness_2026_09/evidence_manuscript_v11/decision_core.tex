\begin{abstract}
Improving average trajectory error does not establish that a learned
forecast should replace a strong motion baseline. We study that
decision on moving-drone trajectories with partial future labels.
We evaluate joint forecast replacement under recording-level
positive-harm and reference-support constraints, distinguishing
forecast availability, candidate retrieval, and causal decisions.
On exposed development data, expanded retrieval leaves 48 recording-seed
views with positive conservative utility over an OLS velocity baseline;
fixing one decoded choice per query leaves 26, and admitting only
choices that pass a predicted risk screen leaves three. In actual
causal policies, exact joint search at the same interventions does
not improve risk, and screening all assignments before choosing
increases risk violations without increasing passes. These findings
identify where candidate capacity is lost. They do not establish a
deployable improvement or independent generalization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Forecast Replacement}
A model can reduce error for some trajectories while worsening
predictions that a motion baseline already handles well. This
asymmetry motivates forecast replacement rather than unconditional
neural forecasting. Here a decision changes a prediction, not an
agent's behavior. We ask whether causal joint choices improve
prediction accuracy without excessive error increases on individual
recordings. Reusing a motion network or solving a larger search is
not, by itself, evidence of that improvement.

Strong constant-velocity comparisons have been emphasized in
pedestrian prediction~\cite{schoeller}. Multi-expert regression
already studies the choice among forecasters~\cite{mao}; deferral
itself is not our contribution. Our focus is the empirical gap
between a useful forecast being available and a past-only joint
decision recovering it under a recording risk constraint.

The observation is eight annotated image positions and the request is
twelve future annotation steps. Neighbor construction, motion features,
and every candidate forecast depend on the current and past positions.
Distances are measured in native image pixels. The trajectory protocol
uses the tracking annotations from MovingDroneCrowd++~\cite{fan};
it is not the publisher's counting or tracking benchmark. The forecast
bank includes causal motion models, a fixed-head EqMotion
adaptation~\cite{eqmotion}, and our Past Context Transformer, with
three frozen forecaster seeds. Causal constant velocity
(CV) is the fallback and the risk reference. OLS4 fits velocity to the
last four positions and is an additional strong accuracy comparator.
Model training and evaluation on this trajectory task do not reproduce
the published best-of-multiple-forecast scores of the original methods.

\section{Risk and Utility With Partial Labels}
For a row with nonempty future support $M_i$, write
$e_i(p)=|M_i|^{-1}\sum_{t\in M_i}\|p_{it}-y_{it}\|_2$.
The baseline error is $b_i=e_i(\mathrm{CV})$ and positive harm is
$[e_i(p)-b_i]_+$. Easy rows have $0<b_i\leq\tau_E$, with
$\tau_E$ fitted before evaluation. For wholly unobserved selected
rows the maximum path disagreement from CV is added to each
positive-harm numerator. Both the all and easy ratios use
unweighted observed reference on selected rows. They require positive
denominators and ratios no greater than 2\%; the signed easy check
uses the entire observed easy reference of the recording. Abstention
does not manufacture a positive selected denominator.

Accuracy uses equal query weights and a common observed CV denominator.
For a comparison between two policies, subtract maximum disagreement
between those policies on wholly unobserved rows from the observed gain.
The resulting lower contrast keeps available-label errors unchanged
on partial rows. It is not a full-grid ADE bound for partial tracks or
a confidence interval. Source-level paired bootstrap intervals average
the three seeds before resampling 14 source groups 3,000 times.

\section{Matched Decisions and Capacity}
Compare independent recipients, a shared forecast family, additive
relative-path costs, and a joint graph cost on the same bank and
proposal counts. The joint cost includes the cross term from changes
in relative paths around an OLS4 anchor; it is not a collision or
physical-safety objective. In the primary development comparison the
four arms each propose 9,675 switches. Table~\ref{tab:matched} gives
their observed risk passes and conservative utility versus OLS4.
The direct paired joint-minus-additive lower contrast is $-0.061$
percentage points with a source interval of $[-0.157,-0.006]$.
This does not support a positive interaction contribution on the
conservative trajectory objective.

To locate the bottleneck, freeze the forecasts, upper switch count
per query, and original scoring rule. A separate hindsight solver
may choose among the legal actions using labels. Relaxing the
recipient roster while keeping the same forecast bank raises
risk-feasible views from 77 to 99 and positive OLS4 views from
39 to 51. This only describes what is possible in the finite bank.
The three infeasible views repeat one recording. Its best distinct
easy replacement has positive harm equal to 23.925\% of its CV
reference in each seed, above the 2\% limit. No reordering of
those candidates can supply the missing easy support.

Next we fit a past-only shortlist on other development sources
using membership in a feasible, positive recording solution as
supervision. At the same eight slots per query it adds three
positive OLS4 views over gain-only retrieval. The improvement
is not conclusive at the source level. The retention comparison
in Figure~\ref{fig:capacity} restricts the solver to the selected
paths and shows losses after retrieval. An exact causal search
changes one query at 72 matched gated switches, leaving four
actual passes. Screening every assignment first produces 2,194
switches and still four passes, while total-risk violations rise
from 12 to 40. In an ungated matched comparison the relative
trajectory improvement on observed pairs is not supported by
the lower bound over missing pair steps.

\paragraph{Small changes can add support without useful accuracy.}
At the same 2,194 switches, a radius fitted from past CV backtests
raises actual recording passes from four to 40, but sacrifices
$0.04553$ points of lower utility relative to the original
predictions and remains below OLS4. With the preregistered
0.25-pixel minimum for applying the cap, none of the operating
paths changes. In the hindsight bank, literal changes make all
102 views risk-feasible; requiring the same minimum displacement
restores the earlier 99. Neither variant adds a verified positive
OLS4 view; three accuracy optimizations in each variant remain
unresolved. The displacement floor is a sensitivity analysis,
not a measured annotation resolution.

\section{Limitations and Independent Evidence}
These are exposed source-held-out experiments, not independent
confirmation. A better shortlist, an optimal solver, and an
apparently feasible predicted margin are distinct from actual
recording-level error control. The current evidence does not
justify replacing the baseline or a claim of metric geometry,
physical safety, or a transferable world model.

An independent evaluation protocol fixes separate DroneCrowd
calibration and confirmation groups on the separate release
of Wen et al.~\cite{wen}. This draft contains no
external result. Confirmation is conditional on the preregistered
calibration criteria; none of the exposed comparisons can be
substituted for it.

\section{Reproducibility}
The evidence tables and vector figure are derived from verified
scalar readouts. The experimental replay uses frozen source roles,
predictions and original per-recording scores. Replaying aggregates
does not reproduce upstream training or add independent evidence.
The submission package is not yet complete.
