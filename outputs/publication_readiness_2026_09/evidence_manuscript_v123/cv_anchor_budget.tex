\section{Edit capacity with CV as the starting forecast}
\label{supp:cv-anchor-budget}
The failure of sparse OLS4 editing does not imply that sparse CV editing
can outperform OLS4. For each row and action, compute the increment in
the query-weighted paired lower gain over leaving CV unchanged. The largest
positive increment per row, sorted within query, gives an exact separate
top-$k$ utility optimum for each reference in the finite menu. This is a
hindsight capacity calculation. It does not constrain or certify any
learned action rule.

The arbitrary-path relaxation can place each edited prediction exactly at
the target on known steps and exactly at the comparison forecast on missing
steps. The latter minimizes the triangle-inequality debit. To tighten the
finite-menu upper bound, let $g(x)$ be the additive gain and $r_a(x),r_e(x)$
the original all and easy positive-harm budget excesses. Any feasible plan
satisfies $r_a,r_e\leq 0$, so for nonnegative $\lambda_a,\lambda_e$,
\[
  g(x) \leq g(x)-\lambda_a r_a(x)-\lambda_e r_e(x).
\]
The right side still maximizes by top-$k$ per query. Minimizing those maxima
over a fixed grid gives a valid ceiling, even after relaxing selected-
reference support and signed easy. No multiplier is used for deployment.

At cap one the CV-only menu optimum is \CVAnchorVsCV{} points against CV,
yet even arbitrary edits give at most \CVAnchorArbitrary{} points against
OLS4 on average. The finite-menu risk-relaxed ceiling is
\CVAnchorTight{} points and no recording has a positive ceiling. Larger
caps expand capacity, but removing the cap still leaves
\CVAnchorUnlimitedFail{} recordings with a nonpositive risk-relaxed ceiling.
Thus accuracy against CV and full-grid non-loss against OLS4 are distinct
requirements. These results neither estimate missing labels nor certify a
policy on independent scenes.
