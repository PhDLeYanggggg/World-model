\section{Sparse Allocation or Distributed Motion?}
On the same OpenDD development cohorts, we fix the incumbent
direction and allocate one of three positive amplitudes online.
Separate models predict support and observed harm from the
past, allowing the unknown debit to scale with the candidate
displacement. The ledger uses predicted quantities only.
Adding a rolling observed footprint does not improve the
original recording-risk pass count over the factorized model
without it.

At identical edit counts and identical realized radius multisets
within every query, joint allocation yields
\AmplitudeJointIndependent{} points vs.\ independent selection
and \AmplitudeJointAdditive{} vs.\ additive geometry; neither
interval supports a utility gain. The joint geometry proxy
improves over independent but not additive selection.

Spreading the joint movement over all available targets recovers
easy reference without adding movement mass. It loses
\AmplitudeSpreadJoint{} points in the paired utility bound;
closely similar CV-relative point estimates do not make that
pairwise lower bound zero. A fixed half-amplitude rule is a
strong simple control. At identical half radii, the earlier
pointwise shape gains \AmplitudeHalfShape{} points, but loses
five risk passes. Non-canceling recording training recovers
four passes but loses utility. No policy meets the complete
requirement, and no external or confirmation outcomes are used.
