# Supported-Pair Failure Analysis

Result source: fresh real-data risk-head fitting and development evaluation,
plus a separately labeled constructed algebraic diagnostic. No new forecaster,
threshold selection, calibration fit or deployment was performed.

| Explanation | What the experiment establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Sparse ranking supervision | Neural/easy pair exposure rose 3.24 times with unchanged minibatches. | Extra pairs did not produce a consistent same-count advantage. Scarcity is not a sufficient explanation of failure. |
| Optimization | Neural/easy fixed total loss fell in 9/9 heads, ranking loss in 8/9. | One fixed fitting batch is not validation, global convergence or proof of enough training. |
| Unintended change outside sparse events | Neural/all score banks and policies are exactly unchanged in all nine groups. | This negative control does not certify every legacy code path. |
| Strong-control degradation | Damping/easy all-ADE worsens in 9/9 points, with seven negative intervals. | A smaller neural-to-damping gap is not necessarily a better neural model. |
| Risk-target mismatch | A constructed example makes the actual ranking loss prefer underestimating an unsafe state. | It does not identify the empirical fraction attributable to this mechanism or optimize the full combined loss. |
| Unsupported zero-reference events | Neural harms zero-CV cases in 11/18 views despite all 18 passing positive-easy percentage limits. | Six views have no zero-CV examples. Their preservation score is not event-specific evidence. |
| Limited independent scene support | All twelve source localities are opened development; every fit sees four. | Three seeds and dependent bootstrap views do not replace independent confirmation. |

## Why This Is Not a Neural Dynamics Result

The trajectory forecasts were fixed. Only risk heads changed. Even an improved
controller would need comparison with an equally trained and protected motion
baseline before claiming a neural forecasting contribution. Here all 18 direct
all-ADE point contrasts favor damping, and 17 conditional intervals exclude zero
in damping's favor. The hard subset has no positive neural interval.

More supervised pairs did change easy-event predictions, but most neural
matched-count intervals include zero. Damping's stronger negative changes show
that the repair is not a general-purpose improvement either. Do not cherry-pick
one seed, one scene rotation or an intervention count after this readout.

## Next Falsifiable Experiment

The current auxiliary ranks sample shares q=H/(B+H). The deployment quantity is
a ratio of conditional moments. [The explicit example](estimand_counterexample.md)
shows why those can disagree even with unlimited exact observations of its two
states. First test a risk-ordering target whose conditional sign matches the
desired moment ratio. Keep supported-first pairs, moment/occurrence losses,
capacity, seeds, updates, forecast banks, source roles and 2% limit fixed.

One option is the signed cross-product H_i B_j-H_j B_i. Its sign identity needs
independent conditional observations, and normalized finite-batch optimization
can behave differently. Before any new real fit, test these assumptions and
weight tails on fitting-only data. Report high variance rather than clipping or
choosing coefficients from the current readout. This proposal is not a trained
repair or a claimed theoretical contribution.

Separately, unsupported zero-error protection needs causal abstention with
source support. Do not memorize opened zero-CV IDs, inspect future errors at
inference or redefine zero error after failure. Do not open reserved confirmation
to compensate for repeated development failures.
