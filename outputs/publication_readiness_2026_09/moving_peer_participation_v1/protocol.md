## Question And Evidence

The previous proposed-action bridge did not improve fitting decisions consistently.
Context1 had1.078% lower MSE but29.496% higher false-safe cost. About76% of its
added decision cost was in one source and a similar share was in single/empty
proposal windows. That treatment changed candidate identity AND excluded
non-proposing peers. The latter can discard background motion. This is a
specific untested hypothesis, not an established cause of failure.

Earlier selected-set and current-policy critics remain negative; this is not
another query-loss rewrite. A bounded source/config/protocol search found no
completed same-active/proposed-all factor contrast. Existing same-all and
proposed-active results are retained exactly, not renamed as fresh fits.

## Complete Factorial Design

| Arm | Peer candidate representation | Peer participation | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| same_action | Same index as ego candidate | All peers | cached_verified |
| same_active | Same index as ego candidate | Only peers proposing intervention | fresh_run |
| proposed_action | Frozen proposed candidate | Only peers proposing intervention | cached_verified |
| proposed_all | Frozen proposed candidate; floor for non-proposers | All peers | fresh_run |

Every average excludes ego and uses complete queries. Empty active-peer sets
produce zero added context; this is a model computation, never a safety pass.
All arms retain the same original context plus an equally sized learned bridge.
Action0 is the causal physical floor: verify that its candidate and floor path
fields agree exactly. It is used for background context only and remains illegal
as an intervention proposal. No future labels, availability or easy truth are
input features. Candidate rollouts are frozen causal forecasts, not observed
future trajectories. Training proposal masks replay from the same source-disjoint
proposer; full legal masks are not treated as proposal masks at inference.

## Positioning And Limits

Set pooling is established prior art, not a novelty claim:
[Deep Sets, NeurIPS2017](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/f22e4747da1aa27e363d86d40ff442fe-Abstract.html).
This factorial experiment tests which causal peer information helps the current
risk estimator. It is not an interaction-dynamics proof, new trajectory model,
JEPA result or a population guarantee. Proposed context still precedes the
final veto. Mean pooling may dilute rare peers; floor-token context must learn
from fitting data despite action0 having no direct intervention-loss support.
Either or both factors may fail, and existing information may be insufficient.
