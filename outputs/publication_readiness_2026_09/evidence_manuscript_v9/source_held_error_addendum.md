# Source-Held Error Discovery: Negative Model-Side Control

I tested whether false-safe errors discovered on a fitting source omitted by
the discovery model provide better supervision than errors on a source seen
by an equal-sized model. Eight source-held discovery networks were trained
from scratch. Each excludes its omitted source from weights and preprocessing.
Six paired students then continued identical kinematic weights for2,000updates,
using either in-source or source-held error masks at the same fixed weight10.
All models froze before the existing exposed SOURCE validation readout.

Known ADE improvement over the physical floor is2.9544% for source-held errors
and3.2049% for in-source errors. The paired difference is-0.2505percentage
points, with missing-outcome completion bounds[-0.8846,+0.3837]. These are
not confidence intervals. Both variants fail complete original risk/support
in all17recording views; the source-held variant reaches64.1321% worst defined
signed easy-net degradation and introduces two undefined selected-easy views.

The treatment reduces loss on its selected error channels but increases
full-fitting false-safe easy signs compared with the control in all three
contexts. On held sources it rejects many beneficial interventions in one
source while increasing beneficial but easy-harming interventions in another.
Error discovery alone does not establish a useful safe-admission boundary.

This result is based on seven exposed source units, one producer seed and
three contexts whose risk-fitting sets contain only two or three sources.
It is a controlled development result, not independent confirmation or a
new neural world-dynamics contribution. The original2%rules, NA handling,
reserved data and all older negative results remain unchanged. Stage5C and
SMC remain disabled; no deployment or submission-readiness upgrade follows.
