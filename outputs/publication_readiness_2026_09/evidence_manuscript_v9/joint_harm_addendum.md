# Joint Event Supervision: Mechanism Improvement Without Admission Success

This is an exposed-SOURCE development addendum, not new independent evidence
or a replacement for a failed primary. It is separate from the compiled main
PDF. The original evaluation rules, 2% risk thresholds, support requirements
and unavailable views remain unchanged. No identity certification was added.

## Controlled Experiment

I trained 108 real Torch heads with 216,000 primary updates. The 54 matched pairs
share architecture, initialization, own-source preprocessing, query draws and
optimizer settings. Only joint-event and conditional-magnitude auxiliary
supervision differs. All models froze before the new nested-C validation.
Both arms retain the same source-disjoint proposer/critic arrangement and
query-level intervention-count/amplitude-matched utility control. All 108
orientation blocks and prediction hashes replay exactly.

## Result

The event arm has 24/276 unavailable nonempty primary views, so the complete
primary remains NA. Its full-strategy known secondary is -1.031 pp
[-1.886,-0.369], and direct lower-1.543pp[-2.811,-0.601] against its matched
control. Event versus product full-policy known difference is+0.275pp
[-0.062,+0.665], with a direct lower difference+0.099pp[-0.243,+0.446]. The
latter comparisons do not match intervention rate and are inconclusive.
Only 47/696 repeated record views satisfy original full-policy risk/support.

All-legal known-action harm and easy-harm fraction MSE improve over the
product-moment control, but occurrence scores are worse than fitting-only
frequency priors. The product model's unconstrained factors are not a strong
probability calibration baseline. On fixed proposer known rows, event-model
H/EH masses are overestimated in aggregate; after veto selection, admitted
H/EH masses are underestimated by factors 5.975/162.400. These are descriptive
ratios over repeated rows, not confidence bounds. They demonstrate that
aggregate conservatism and selected-set reliability are different questions;
they do not isolate a unique causal explanation or prove a new theorem.

Intervals use 3,000 source-group resamples after context/seed/orientation
averaging, conditional on 23 exposed source units. Native observation 8 and
prediction 12 use annotation order and image pixels, not verified video time
or metric coordinates. The original 24-group primary remains not estimable.

## Consequence For The Paper

The factorized supervision treatment is not a successful main-method claim.
It is a bounded negative experiment with a more specific diagnostic of
selection-dependent risk error. The published multi-expert deferral loss
primitive has equation-level tests but no completed task-matched real-data
comparison yet. That gap, independent confirmation and a safe useful policy
remain unresolved. No deployment, Stage5C, SMC or submission readiness follows.
