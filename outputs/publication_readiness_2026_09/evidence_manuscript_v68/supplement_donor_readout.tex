\paragraph{Donor-only calibration of action risk and gain.}
We hold both the outer location and a calibration donor out of each
factorized moment fit. For each training location in that fit, its
nomination ranker also excludes itself. On predictions from the excluded
donors, we fit the sign probability and conditional positive size of
each risk component, with equal total donor weights. The soft policy
penalizes their product. It gives \DonorReadoutMatched{} versus
\DonorReadoutMatchedFactor{} of \DonorReadoutViews{} joint passes at
matched realized counts, and \DonorReadoutOnline{} versus
\DonorReadoutOnlineFactor{} online. At threshold 0.5 the easy-risk
probability gives \DonorReadoutFpr{}\% false positives and
\DonorReadoutRecall{}\% recall. Its Brier score is \DonorReadoutBrier{}
versus \DonorReadoutPrior{} for donor prevalence. A separately fitted
gain readout lowers error but loses \DonorReadoutGain{} pp to the causal
prefix policy (95\% location interval [\DonorReadoutGainLow{},
\DonorReadoutGainHigh{}]). No controller qualifies under the original
rule; these locations are development evidence only.

\paragraph{A recording ratio need not be monotone in a threshold.}
Consider three easy nonidentity edits, each with reference error one and
positive harm $0.03$, $0$, and $0.06$ in score order. Nested selected sets
of sizes one, two and three have ratios $3\%$, $1.5\%$, and $3\%$.
Only the middle set passes the original recording evaluator; selecting
nothing is undefined. This example with complete labels shows why we do
not infer monotone risk from a nested edit set or attach a monotone-loss
conformal guarantee~\cite{crc} to the donor probability readout.
