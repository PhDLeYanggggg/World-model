# Paper Addendum: Source Calibration Is Not Sufficient

We tested whether fitting-excluded source calibration and an input-support
screen could recover useful neural interventions. Nine frozen forecasting banks
and two fixed risk-objective families were evaluated under three seeds. Each
producer and controller used disjoint four-locality rosters; the remaining four
localities were partitioned into two calibration and two evaluation sources in
all six fixed rotations. No evaluation outcome selected its corresponding
threshold. All model and preprocessing producers excluded both latter roles.

The primary signed-score calibrated-supported neural policy underperformed
equally protected damping by0.670% ADE (locality interval[-0.974,-0.396] for
neural gain). Neural gain over constant velocity was0.167%, compared with0.826%
for damping. All three seed intervals favored damping. Although easy error was
preserved in every evaluated view and reference-exact cases were untouched,
nine of216 dependent neural views exceeded the2% selected positive-harm budget.
Generic support filtering reduced useful actions without eliminating risk.

These source-development results show that empirical calibration can trade
accuracy and intervention coverage for better observed easy preservation without
establishing a neural advantage. They do not provide independent confirmation,
conformal risk control or physical safety. Learn then Test treats risk control
as statistical testing; original Conformal Risk Control covers monotone losses.
Our changing-subset ratio and two-source empirical grid do not implement those
guarantees. [Learn then Test](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01052),
[Conformal Risk Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02814).

All observations are detector-derived silver image-local trajectories with
8 observed and12 predicted steps at raw-frame stride12. These results neither
recertify historical Stage37 claims nor support metric/seconds, true3D,
foundation-model or submission-readiness claims.
