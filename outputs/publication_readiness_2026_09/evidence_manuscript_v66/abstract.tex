Lower mean trajectory error does not establish when a learned forecast
should replace a strong motion baseline. We audit this decision under
partial future labels and recording-level positive-harm constraints.
Matched interventions separate motion budget from recipient choice,
and label-aware optimization separates feasible actions from actions
recoverable by a past-only policy. On OpenDD development data, even
with fixed nominations and chronological credits, \DirectCountStrict{}
of \DirectCountViews{} recording--seed views admit a verified action
plan that passes risk and beats constant velocity and OLS4. The online
count policy succeeds in \DirectCountCount{} views. Learning direct
gain and harm shows some discrimination, but adding peer histories
does not improve matched utility. On 40 separate DroneCrowd calibration
recordings, no frozen threshold qualifies and OLS4 has substantially
lower error than the tested joint controller. These results quantify
where causal intervention falls short; they do not establish reliable
generalization. Independent confirmation remains sealed.
