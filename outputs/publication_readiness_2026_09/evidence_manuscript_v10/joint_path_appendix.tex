{\Large\bfseries Joint Path Accuracy Under Missing Pair Outcomes}

\textbf{Development appendix.} We use 34 exposed recordings in 14 source
groups, 1,613 queries and three frozen forecaster seeds. The four learned
full-amplitude policies share 9,675 interventions at identical query
counts. CV and fitting-selected OLS4 are reference forecasts, not
count-matched policies. The 102 recording views are not independent.

\textbf{Pair outcomes.} For agents $i,j$, let $r_{a,t}$ be the relative
vector predicted by arm $a$ and $r_t$ the labeled vector. Edges are the
undirected union of the four nearest neighbors at the last observed
frame. A pair-step is observed only when both agents are labeled:
9,222,204/11,361,600 pair-steps and 647,949/946,800 terminal pairs.
For a comparison $a$ vs. $b$, define
$d_t=\lVert r_{b,t}-r_t\rVert_2-\lVert r_{a,t}-r_t\rVert_2$,
so positive values favor $a$. At a missing pair-step,
\[
 -\lVert r_{a,t}-r_{b,t}\rVert_2 \leq d_t
 \leq \lVert r_{a,t}-r_{b,t}\rVert_2.
\]
These bounds are averaged with the exact observed differences over
the requested pair grid within each query. We average queries, then
seeds and sources, and bootstrap the 14 sources 3,000 times.

\textbf{Interpretation.} Joint selection improves on independent
selection on observed pairs, but the matched additive control and
missing-label analysis do not support a separate gain for the
pair interaction term. OLS4 improves observed pair ADE (28.842 vs.
32.618 pixels) but worsens available scene ADE (48.012 vs. 45.423
pixels) relative to joint selection. Relative geometry and absolute
scene accuracy need not agree. The p95 query pair error is 51.396
for joint and 49.805 for CV; the corresponding worst recording
views are 80.804 and 79.686. Neither calibrated sparse policy
demonstrates a strictly positive lower-bound source interval.
This analysis leaves the original risk rule unchanged and does not
measure physical collision safety or establish independent confirmation.
