\section{Why The Matched Recording Policies Fail}
\label{sec:matched-risk}
We replayed the matched-count development choices without changing
models, labels, thresholds, or recording rules. An observed failure
means that a defined ratio already exceeded 2\% before charging
wholly unobserved switches, or that signed easy degradation exceeded
2\%. A debit failure means that a defined positive-harm ratio first
exceeds 2\% after charging disagreement on wholly unobserved rows.
An undefined check has no positive reference denominator. We record
each view once in Table~\ref{tab:frozen-risk}, giving observed failures
precedence even when another check is debit-driven or undefined.
For example, 98 of 102 full-bank student views have at least one
observed failure. One has a debit failure only, and three fail only
for undefined support. The missing-label debit widens the utility
envelope but does not explain away most recording failures.

The completion envelope in Table~\ref{tab:frozen-utility}
straddles zero for both students. Their negative lower bounds
persist under every single-source omission, but are not proof
that every completion loses. The full-bank student makes 1,394
switches without any future labels, versus 133 for gain ranking.
Those counts are repeated forecast rows, not people or independent
observations. They describe where each policy intervened; they
do not identify the errors of the unlabeled forecasts.
