\paragraph{Learning swaps under exactly matched counts.}
We freeze the previous hurdle's per-query counts and fit 63 native Torch
heads on the same development locations over three seeds. Each training
example compares two legal configurations at the same nonzero count.
The training target combines local two-reference gain, signed CV-relative
risk and an easy-support term, rather than claiming local risk equals the
recording gate. Joint ranking adds a symmetric pair term to per-action
values. With \MatchedSwapCount{} edits per arm, risk passes with gain
over both references are \MatchedSwapIndependent{}/\MatchedSwapViews{}
for independent utility, \MatchedSwapPointFit{} for joint point regression,
\MatchedSwapAdditive{} for additive ranking and \MatchedSwapJoint{} for
joint ranking. The joint direct gain over independent choice is
\MatchedSwapEffect{} pp (location-bootstrap 95\% interval
[\MatchedSwapLow{}, \MatchedSwapHigh{}]); omitting one two-recording
location as a sensitivity check leaves \MatchedSwapWithout{} pp.
We find no reliable benefit of the pair term. Label-aware optimization
at the \emph{exact} frozen counts verifies \MatchedSwapCapacity{} cases
with risk passes and dual gain, versus 510 on this recording-seed grid
when only the count cap applies. At the frozen counts, \MatchedSwapNoEdit{}
cases have no switches and \MatchedSwapNoEasy{} cannot reach a legal easy
action despite having easy references. This is evidence of restrictions
from both the frozen count allocation and the learned choices, not an
independent confirmation or a safety claim.
