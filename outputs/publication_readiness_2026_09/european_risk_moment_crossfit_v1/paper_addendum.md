# Development Evidence: Selection-Conditional Risk Miscalibration

We investigate why an improved neural forecaster loses its advantage when
screened by learned positive-harm/reference-error moments. On twelve opened
training-source localities,144 matched heads provide three-seed diagnostics
with a three-source fitting and one-source holdout design. The forecast bank
and causal feature schema are frozen. Independent roles remain closed.

The neural controller reduces held-source reference-cost and positive-harm MSE
over training constants by13.41% [3.75,22.14] and14.88% [10.74,19.01]. Nevertheless,
the subset satisfying a predicted2% relative-harm constraint has observed
positive-harm/reference ratios of2.85% [2.38,3.29] in fitting sources and4.87%
[3.19,6.92] in held sources. Reference-error inflation and harm underestimation
coexist in that subset. Marginal prediction skill therefore does not establish
the reliability of this selection rule.

Intervals use3000 locality bootstrap draws after averaging dependent seed and
producer views within each locality. They are exploratory source-development
intervals, not independent confirmation. This diagnostic omits the complete
utility/easy policy and makes no deployment or physical-safety claim. Positive
harm is not net ADE degradation. The result motivates a controlled risk-budget
objective and nested source calibration; neither repair has been demonstrated
by this experiment. These observations do not by themselves establish novelty
or submission readiness.
