\subsection{Deferring the gain edit to the first eligible query}
At the original gain trigger, a head predicts the realized utility
difference between acting now and waiting up to eight query indices for
the first radius-matched neural offer after support is observed.
It also predicts whether waiting loses a defined reference, a risk pass,
or a joint pass (one deterioration target), and whether it expires.
The support edit is unchanged. The head sees the observed prefix only;
its targets are the outcomes of the two causal policies, not the best
later outcome. A recording with an expired gain retains only its original
support edit. A retrospective immediate control uses the same realized
gain and support counts per recording.

The guarded head waits \GainWaitWaited{} times, with
\GainWaitGuardExpired{} expiries. Its conservative utility differences
are \GainWaitPrimaryEffect{} against the original and
\GainWaitMatchedEffect{} against immediate use at the same realized
budget. Observed context versus the retrained anchor-only guard gives
\GainWaitContextEffect{} (paired location bootstrap).

Under always waiting, \GainWaitExpired{} of \GainWaitReleases{} triggers
expire: \GainWaitNever{} never observe a support edit,
\GainWaitLate{} observe it too late, and \GainWaitRadius{} lack a
radius-matched neural offer. An outcome-aware comparison of immediate
and first eligible actions admits \GainWaitFirst{} views, compared with
\GainWaitNeural{} when any neural offer is allowed in the same window,
and \GainWaitExpanded{} when OLS4 and observed-prefix directions are
also allowed. All ceilings preserve existing easy references. More
directions can address some matching failures, but not absent support.
No learned timing rule qualifies for deployment or independent confirmation.
