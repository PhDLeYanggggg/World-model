\paragraph{Ranking exchanges that compete for a recording budget.}
Prior work ranks feasible solutions for decision-focused
learning~\cite{mandi2022rank}. We use this idea for forecast exchanges
under partial labels. An exchange moves the same number of edits between
two queries and must respect chronological credit. For observed gain
difference $d$ and the summed forecast disagreement $u$ at missing steps,
the triangle inequality bounds the difference by $[d-u,d+u]$. We use the
signed margin $\operatorname{sgn}(d)\max(|d|-u,0)$ and omit ambiguous
directions from the ranking loss. The point and swap heads share their
architecture, point objective, candidate bank and frozen risk terms.
Nominations and anchor scores for a donor exclude both that donor and
the outer location.

In 42 native Torch fits, swap ranking gives \CrossSwapMatchedRank{}
versus \CrossSwapMatchedPoint{} joint passes at \CrossSwapEdits{} matched
edits, compared with \CrossSwapMatchedFactor{} for frozen factorized
risk (\CrossSwapViews{} recording--seed views). Its direct difference
to point-only is \CrossSwapDiff{} pp (95\% location interval
[\CrossSwapLow{}, \CrossSwapHigh{}]). Online passes are \CrossSwapOnlineRank{}
versus \CrossSwapOnlinePoint{}. Of \CrossSwapPairN{} held pairs,
\CrossSwapCert{}\% have an identifiable direction under the bound;
concordance is \CrossSwapAccRank{}\% for the swap head and
\CrossSwapAccPoint{}\% for point-only. The pairs are dependent. Neither
the gain nor the ranking improves; missing directions alone do not
explain this result. Confirmation remains unopened.
