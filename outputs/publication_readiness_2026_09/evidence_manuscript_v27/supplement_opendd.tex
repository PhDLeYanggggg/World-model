\section{A Better-Labeled Development Source}
To separate missing-label effects from decision errors, we study
the OpenDD v3 pedestrian tracks~\cite{opendd}.
We use eight observations and twelve forecast samples at stride 12
on its released timestamp grid, rather than the main experiment's
stride-one image-pixel contract. Data are reported in publisher
projected coordinates. The cohort has 10,475 pedestrian targets
in 3,395 non-overlapping intervals from 202 flights, with 93.78\%
complete future paths. All seven roundabouts are evaluated by
location exclusion. They are not independent cities. This source
had already informed development, and we do not use it as a new
confirmation set.

With CV fallback and five fixed causal alternatives, we fit three
models: pointwise conservative gain regression, the same regression
with expected within-query regret, and regret with extra OLS4 harm.
The comparison shares the forecast menu, model size, draws, and
exact intervention counts. In the two restricted menus, a past-only
hindcast rule fixes either the candidate for each person or the
person to edit. All 63 fits use the same budget, across three seeds
and seven held locations.

Training for within-query regret does not establish a gain over
pointwise regression. The dual-reference model at 20\% coverage
limits the worst descriptive easy increase to \OpenDDLowDualEasy\%,
but does not improve on CV. Positive hindsight values in both
restricted menus suggest that either decision could improve, but
not that the improvements are predictable with these inputs. This
diagnostic neither tests joint multi-edit interaction nor relaxes
the original per-recording risk rule. The external confirmation
groups remain unopened.
