# Directional Peer-Path Information Probe

We test whether stepwise directional relations to nearby agents add predictive
information beyond existing history, rollout and proximity summaries. A fixed
source-held design compares120-dimensional summaries,180-dimensional aligned
features, and an equal-width within-query misassociation control. ExtraTrees is
the registered primary probe and ridge the secondary. Normalization and target
scales are learned only on the fitting sources. The forecasts and development
sources were previously exposed; this is not independent confirmation.

Across three fitting contexts, aligned features reduce primary normalized risk
MSE by-1.262%,+0.997%,+5.000%. Corresponding joint-cost reductions are-0.000449%,
+0.189098%,0%. The fixed follow-up screen passes only1/3. In the favorable third
context all38,579 known candidates remain rejected, so improved continuous
prediction does not improve decisions. In the second context admissions rise
10to61 and harmful admissions1to10. Absolute costs and all eight source-fold
results are provided, including the44-row source. No secondary comparison or
rounded percentage is substituted for the primary criterion.

All48 fits and deterministic refits reproduce exactly, including eight repeated
legacy ridge controls. These probes are not neural dynamics training, proof
of safe deployment, or independent seed replication. Unknown outcomes remain
unknown. The original2% harm, easy-net and support rules, previous failures and
unavailable original24-group primary remain unchanged. No identity exemption,
new reserved evaluation or deployment is introduced.

The result supports a narrow distinction: added geometry can improve risk
regression without improving the admission decision. It does not prove that
interaction information is generally useless or that a new loss will repair
the problem. This addendum is separate from the unchanged compiled v9 paper.
